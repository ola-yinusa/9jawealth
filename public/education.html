Nigerian Land Title & Fee Glossary

Before paying a deposit anywhere in Lagos or Ogun State, make sure you understand the exact legal title on offer and which statutory or estate fees are separate from the headline land price.

Certificate of Occupancy (C of O)
A statutory land title granted by a State Governor confirming the holder's right of occupancy for a fixed term (commonly up to 99 years). Always ask to inspect the verified file number or gazette reference rather than relying on verbal “C of O in process” assurances.
Governor's Consent
Under Nigeria's Land Use Act, once land already carries a Certificate of Occupancy or prior state title, any subsequent sale or transfer requires the State Governor's Consent to legally perfect the new buyer's ownership interest.
Registered Survey Plan
A technical boundary plan prepared by a licensed surveyor and lodged at the Office of the Surveyor-General. Its coordinates allow an independent surveyor or lawyer to chart the land and confirm it sits outside committed government acquisition or road setbacks.
Deed of Assignment
The formal legal contract transferring title from the seller or developer to you after full payment. At PalmVille, completing payment entitles you to a Registered Survey and Deed of Assignment in your individual or corporate name (corporate registration attracts extra statutory fees).
Preliminary Development Fee vs. Main Infrastructure Fee
In residential estates like Lekki Avana Signature, published all-in plot totals cover Land, Survey Plan, Legal Fee, and the Preliminary Development Fee (initial perimeter fencing and gatehouse works). A separate Main Infrastructure Fee applies later prior to building to fund internal roads, drainage, and estate utilities, so always confirm both figures before budgeting.
Diaspora Remote Verification
Overseas investors should verify properties remotely by: (1) requesting survey coordinates for independent charting at the state land registry, (2) reading refund and default clauses in the contract before paying, (3) sending an independent representative or lawyer to physical allocation and site checks, and (4) transferring funds strictly to verified corporate accounts.

Why Nigerian Real Estate Remains Africa's Most Compelling Opportunity

Understanding the structural backdrop helps you separate long-term asset selection from short-term market noise.

220M+
Population Driving Demand

Nigeria has Africa's largest population and continues to urbanise steadily. A housing deficit exceeding 20 million units creates durable demand that outlasts short-term economic cycles.

₦+
Inflation & Naira Hedge

Well-located land and productive real assets help preserve purchasing power against naira depreciation, holding real utility across currency and inflation shifts.

3–10×
10-Year Growth Corridors

Disciplined acquisitions in verified corridors across Lagos peri-urban zones, Ogun industrial belts, and FCT expansion districts have historically compounded meaningfully over a decade.

The clearest lesson for Nigerian investors: titled land in verified growth corridors rarely loses nominal value over long holding periods. Slowdowns are repeatedly followed by repricing once roads, power, and population catch up. The real edge comes from verified title, honest budgeting, and patience.
