The System is Working. Your Salary Lasts. So Why Does the Money Still Feel Like It is Going Nowhere?

Something interesting happens to a man after he fixes his salary structure. The borrowing stops. The shame lifts. The 20th of the month arrives and the account still has something in it. His wife notices. He sleeps better.

And then, quietly, a new question begins to form.

He looks at the savings growing slowly in the account. He checks the balance. He does the maths. And he realises something that nobody told him about: the naira he is carefully holding is losing value faster than the account is growing it.

He did everything right. He fixed the structure. He started saving. And inflation is still winning.

Treasury bills are yielding somewhere around 18 to 22 percent. Inflation is running above 30 percent. A standard savings account is offering 4 percent. Which means the man who saves in the wrong place is not just standing still. He is moving backwards in real terms. Every month. Quietly.

His wife asks: when are we buying land? When are we going on that trip? When do we stop remaining on the same spot?

He has no answer. Not because he is irresponsible. But because nobody showed him what comes after the budgeting. The financial content he has consumed either assumes he already has a dollar account and a stock portfolio, or it is so vague it tells him nothing specific.

He can see other men moving forward. Taking on property. Building positions quietly. And he is saving diligently in a savings account that is slowly shrinking in real value.

He is no longer the man who cannot save. He is the man who saves but cannot grow. And that new identity is, in many ways, more frustrating than the first one.

The naira is a leaking bucket. You cannot store water in a leaking bucket forever. Once the discipline is in place, the next job is to move the surplus into things that grow faster than inflation moves. Not speculation. Not gambling.
